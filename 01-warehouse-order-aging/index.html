Business findings & recommended actions

1. Prioritize the Frozen zone. Frozen has a 31.7% late-shipment rate (1,027 of 3,239 shipped orders) and accounts for about 74% of all late shipments. Review its order queue, staging process, cutoff times, and staffing by shift before expanding capacity.

2. Investigate the Friday handoff. Friday orders have the highest late rate at 19.5%, followed by Saturday at 10.9%. Compare order cutoffs, weekend staffing, and promised ship-by dates to determine whether orders are waiting through the weekend.

3. Protect the standard service level. Standard orders are 7.6% late, compared with 0% for expedited orders in this simulated dataset. Check whether the two service levels use different queue rules or processing targets, and validate that the difference is operationally sustainable.

4. Monitor weekly changes. The historical weekly late rate ranged from 0% to 15.4%, with several weeks around 10% to 13% in May and early June. Set a weekly review threshold, investigate sustained increases, and compare the pattern with staffing, volume, and cutoff changes.

Suggested first step: run a focused Frozen-zone and Friday-order review, then track late rate and same-day shipping weekly to see whether changes improve performance.

These are hypotheses and recommendations based on simulated data, not proof of the cause of delays. The dataset does not contain staffing hours or direct capacity measures.

Method: a shipment is classified as late when its shipped date is later than its ship-by date. Records without a shipped date are excluded from the shipped-order rate and average transit-time calculation. Dataset and results are illustrative, not real employer data.
